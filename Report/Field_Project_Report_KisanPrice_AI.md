## CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Background of Indian Agricultural Marketing
Agriculture remains the principal source of livelihood for over 58% of India's population. In Maharashtra, cash crops such as Onion, Soybean, Cotton, Sugarcane, and Pomegranate represent vital economic lifelines. Despite rapid advancements in digital commerce, the wholesale marketing of agricultural commodities continues to be governed by physical wholesale yards known as **Agricultural Produce Market Committees (APMC)** established under state marketing legislations.

Under the APMC mechanism, farmers bring harvested produce to designated yards where registered commission agents (*Adatdars*) conduct open auctions with licensed wholesale buyers. However, the physical process suffers from structural inefficiencies:
* **Localized Price Secrecy**: Prevailing price trends are known primarily to traders inside the mandi premises.
* **Delayed Official Reporting**: National portals like Agmarknet frequently experience a 24- to 48-hour lag in publishing verified modal prices.
* **Inelastic Transport Decisions**: Once a farmer arrives at a specific mandi with a loaded truck or tractor, they have zero bargaining power and must sell at whatever price is offered to avoid return transport losses and perishable decay.

### 1.2 The APMC Mandi Ecosystem in Maharashtra
Maharashtra hosts some of Asia's largest single-commodity wholesale markets:
1. **Lasalgaon APMC (Nashik District)**: The benchmark price-setting hub for domestic and export onions in India.
2. **Pune Gultekdi APMC (Pune District)**: The primary consumption and redistribution terminal for western Maharashtra.
3. **Vashi APMC (Navi Mumbai)**: The mega-terminal market catering to the Mumbai metropolitan region, where prices typically command a 12% to 22% premium over producer-district mandis.
4. **Sangli APMC**: Renowned for Turmeric and Raisin auctions.
5. **Kalamna APMC (Nagpur)**: Central India's trading hub for Oranges, Cotton, and Soybean.

Price differentials between these mandis can vary between ₹300 and ₹900 per quintal on any single morning. Farmers lack accessible tools to calculate whether transporting crops to a distant terminal market will result in higher net profits after deducting transport fuel and loading expenses.

### 1.3 Overview of KisanPrice AI
**KisanPrice AI** is an intelligent, full-stack agricultural decision support platform engineered using modern web technologies (Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and Drizzle ORM). It bridges the information divide by providing:
1. **Multi-Mandi Surveillance**: Simultaneous real-time rate comparison across 16 major APMC mandis.
2. **104-Commodity Catalog**: Comprehensive coverage spanning 10 agricultural categories (Vegetables, Fruits, Cereals, Pulses, Oilseeds, Spices, Commercial, Plantation, Fiber, and Other Crops).
3. **100% Vector SVG Visual Identity**: Zero reliance on external photo CDNs, ensuring instantaneous loading and zero broken images on rural mobile networks.
4. **Inter-Mandi Arbitrage & Transit Calculator**: Instant computation of gross price spreads, customizable freight deductions per 100 km, and automated recommendation of the most profitable market.
5. **7-Day Econometric Price Forecast**: Directional trend projections (Bullish / Bearish / Range-Bound) backed by 4 fundamental agricultural market drivers.
6. **Geolocation Engine**: Automatic nearest APMC detection with distance calculation via the Haversine formula.

## CHAPTER 3: ANALYSIS OF EXISTING SYSTEM

### 3.1 Existing Mandi Price Discovery Workflow
The traditional price discovery workflow operates through four manual steps:
1. **Harvesting & Loading**: Produce is harvested and loaded onto transport vehicles without verified knowledge of current market prices.
2. **Arrival & Token Generation**: The vehicle arrives at the APMC yard between 4:00 AM and 6:30 AM. A gate entry slip is issued.
3. **Open Auction (*Boli*)**: The commission agent calls an open auction. Bids are called orally or through manual chalkboard entries.
4. **Weighing & Settlement**: The produce is weighed, deductions for commission (6%), handling, and market cess are calculated on a manual paper bill (*Patti*), and payment is made in cash or via RTGS within 48 hours.

### 3.2 Limitations and Critical Flaws of Current System
1. **Severe Information Asymmetry**: The buyer and agent have access to terminal demand data; the grower is completely blind to regional market trends.
2. **Intermediary Price Skimming**: Intermediaries capture substantial profits by purchasing low in producing districts and selling high in terminal consumption hubs (such as Mumbai or Pune).
3. **Lack of Transit Cost Optimization**: Farmers lack mathematical models to weigh distance against price spread. Often, traveling 40 km further for a ₹50 higher rate leads to net financial loss due to additional vehicle hire charges.
4. **No Predictive Early Warning**: When prices are about to decline due to monsoon arrivals from southern states (e.g., Karnataka tomato arrivals), local farmers receive no warning and experience price crashes.

### 3.3 Comparative Gap Analysis

## CHAPTER 7: TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION

### 7.1 Technology Stack Justification
* **Next.js 16 (App Router)**: Enables hybrid rendering. Server components pre-render initial mandi data for instantaneous first-contentful-paint, while API route handlers process requests without managing external Express servers.
* **React 19 & TypeScript 5.7**: Type-safety ensures mathematical calculations (e.g. price divisions, modal averages, freight calculations) are immune to runtime `NaN` errors.
* **Tailwind CSS v4**: Generates minimal CSS overhead with fluid responsive typography, essential for fast loading on 3G rural mobile devices.
* **Drizzle ORM & PostgreSQL**: Type-safe relational querying with zero overhead, outperforming heavy ORMs like Prisma.
* **Recharts 3.10**: Client-side SVG charting engine rendering smooth price trends and confidence areas with touch-friendly tooltips.

### 7.2 Core Algorithms & Mathematical Formulations

#### 1. Geodesic Proximity: The Haversine Formula
To compute the exact ground distance between the farmer's GPS coordinates and each APMC market:

$$\Delta\text{lat} = \frac{(\text{lat}_2 - \text{lat}_1) \cdot \pi}{180}$$

$$\Delta\text{lon} = \frac{(\text{lon}_2 - \text{lon}_1) \cdot \pi}{180}$$

$$a = \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\text{lat}}{2}\right) + \cos\left(\frac{\text{lat}_1 \cdot \pi}{180}\right) \cdot \cos\left(\frac{\text{lat}_2 \cdot \pi}{180}\right) \cdot \sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\text{lon}}{2}\right)$$

$$c = 2 \cdot \arctan2\left(\sqrt{a}, \sqrt{1-a}\right)$$

$$d = R \cdot c \quad (\text{where } R = 6371\text{ km})$$

#### 2. Net-In-Hand Arbitrage Formula
To determine the true financial advantage of traveling to a distant terminal market:

$$\text{Freight Expense} = \left(\frac{d}{100}\right) \cdot C_{\text{transit}}$$

$$\text{Net Return per Quintal} = P_{\text{modal}} - \text{Freight Expense}$$

$$\text{Arbitrage Advantage} = \text{Net Return}_{\text{Terminal}} - \text{Net Return}_{\text{Local}}$$

Where:
* $P_{\text{modal}}$: Mandi modal auction price per quintal.
* $d$: Distance from origin market in kilometers.
* $C_{\text{transit}}$: User-configurable freight transit parameter (₹/quintal/100 km).
